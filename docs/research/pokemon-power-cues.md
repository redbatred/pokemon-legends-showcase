# Pokémon attack effect research

Research date: 2026-10-27. Scope: four priority species, supplied genuine GLBs, current `PokemonModel`, `PokemonActor`, `PokemonPower`, `powerProfiles` and `SpineReveal`. No runtime code was changed.

## Ready-to-use metadata

`public/assets/pokemon-3d/power-cues.json` is keyed by `charizard`, `blastoise`, `pikachu`, and `greninja`. Each entry supplies the exact existing clip name, emitter bone names and local `tip`/`forward` points, plus suggested source-clip `seek`, `powerAt`, `sustainEnd`, `end`, and `recoverAt` times. `speed` is playback rate. `forward` is a point, not a direction vector. Charge wall time is `(powerAt - seek) / speed`; effect wall duration is `(end - powerAt) / speed`. Do not apply an unrelated duration clamp without adjusting playback speed to preserve the intended pose.

These are measured anatomical sockets and authored effect recommendations. The supplied clips contain generic attack body motion, not officially named move-effect event tracks. Numeric socket precision does not eliminate the need for a final visual alignment check, especially Charizard's static head-relative mouth center while its jaw moves.

## Priority implementation corrections

1. **Project two world points through the same camera.** Transform each socket's local tip and forward point through its actual bone matrix, project both to camera NDC, convert each to the portrait's local pixels, then convert both through Pixi's display transforms into the effect container. Direction is the normalized difference of those final two points. The inspected implementation projects only jaw/head position, ignores `profile.bone`, and then uses a fixed screen angle. Blastoise consequently fires from its face; all actors can fire against their actual pose.
2. **Use rendered-frame sockets.** The mixer advances every tick while the canvas texture renders at 30 fps. Calling `getWorldPosition()` on an intervening tick advances the projected socket beyond the displayed pose. Cache emitter projections when rendering the model, or update the canvas and effects at the same cadence. Greninja's measured hand velocity is 27.3 world units/second near release, so even one skipped frame is visible.
3. **Preserve foreshortening.** The current three-quarter camera is offset `(0.70, 0.22, 1.75) × span`. Global +Z projects mostly left and downward, not to a universal upper-left angle. The attack clips also turn and pitch the body. If the projected axis becomes very short because a muzzle points toward the camera, render an expanding near-camera burst/cone; do not normalize that tiny vector into an arbitrary 245px beam. Keep world direction and projection depth available to the VFX.
4. **Fit the active animation, then hold the camera stable.** Current framing is derived only from idle t=0. Pikachu's jump/flip and Charizard's raised wings exceed their idle silhouettes. Sample idle plus active attack poses while loading, build a conservative union in camera space, add padding, and keep that framing stable during playback. Re-fitting every frame creates visible zoom pumping.
5. **Align envelopes to action time.** The current fixed 1.02-second effect fades after Charizard's open-jaw window and can persist after shorter attacks. Use each action's clip time for emission gates, with bounded per-move tails. `attackTimeScale` is currently ignored by the inspected `play()` implementation in favor of hardcoded 1.4.
6. **Keep launched particles detached.** A continuous stream should emit particles/ribbons at the mouth/nozzle's current pose, then advance them from their birth pose. Rotating the entire existing plume with the head produces a stiff paddle. Greninja's shuriken must capture its launch origin and direction once, then leave the hand.

Three's official [Object3D documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Object3D.html) covers local-to-world transforms; [Vector3](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Vector3.html) covers camera projection. Its [AnimationAction documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/AnimationAction.html) distinguishes clip-local `time` from playback `timeScale`. The installed implementation was also inspected directly.

## Species-specific treatment

**Charizard:** The metadata tip is the mean of the four upper/lower lip bone locations, expressed in `head` space at peak jaw opening. The axis follows `head → center_nose`. This source attack leans forward and down during emission. Use a tight white-yellow throat core, a widening orange flame envelope, irregular tongues and a few detached embers. Start with a mouth-sized width; widen downrange and break up the tail. The current symmetric sine-width shape closes at both ends and reads as a leaf-shaped paddle. Official [Pokkén move documentation](https://assets.pokemon.com/assets/cms2/pdf/video-game/pokken/Charizard_Chart_EN.pdf) lists Flamethrower for Charizard; the exact plume geometry here is an authored interpretation.

**Blastoise:** The terminal cannon bones are `left_feeler_b_02` and `right_feeler_b_02`. Each has 177 fully weighted native cannon vertices. In bone-local coordinates the barrels extend to X=0.26094 and have roughly ±0.083 Y/Z radius. Thus `[0.26,0,0]` is the actual nozzle plane and `[0.52,0,0]` establishes +X firing direction. Render two compact white-blue pressured cores, thin outer blue sheets, edge spray and separate muzzle rings. Keep centerlines directed by the barrels; put turbulence on the surface instead of snaking the whole jet. The official [UNITE Blastoise article](https://unite.pokemon.com/en-gb/news/blastoise-has-blasted-into-pokemon-unite/) describes Hydro Pump as a high-volume water blast. Twin muzzle placement comes from this model's geometry.

**Pikachu:** The two sockets are native red-cheek vertex centroids, with 14 vertices per side and exact bilateral symmetry in head space. `pointlight1/2` are near the eyes and should not substitute for cheeks. Build small cheek sparks during charge; at discharge use 2–3 brief forked bolts directed outward/forward, with a bright core and restrained yellow surround. Seed branching deterministically per pulse so topology remains stable between pulses. The current evenly spaced radial rays resemble a rotating wheel and originate too centrally. Pokémon's [official Pokédex](https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokedex/pikachu) identifies the cheek electric sacs; [UNITE's official Pikachu page](https://unite.pokemon.com/en-us/pokemon/pikachu/) describes ranged electricity attacks.

**Greninja:** The real throwing limb is `left_hand_24`. The tip is the average first finger-joint position converted into wrist space. Its forward point uses the measured hand velocity around the release pose. Charge a compact rotating four-blade water star at the hand; release once near 0.667, then spin it around its own travel axis with a thin water ribbon and short droplet trail. Capture a launch basis once rather than following subsequent wrist motion. Avoid the shared mouth-water stream. The [official Greninja Pokédex](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokedex/greninja?mo_ar=true&so_ar=true) describes compressed-water throwing stars; [UNITE's official page](https://unite.pokemon.com/en-us/pokemon/greninja/) describes a large thrown water shuriken. One clean projectile best matches the supplied single throw; multiple successive throws would need additional matching motion.

## Evidence and limits

Sampling used the exact currently shipped GLBs and source-preserved nodes/skins. The local research folder `/tmp/pokemon-power-research/` contains 60Hz bone position samples, socket derivation, a 28-pose contact sheet, and projection audit. `docs/research/pokemon-power-projection-audit.json` preserves the sampled projected extents. The visible texture range is NDC -1 to +1. Across the inspected poses Charizard reaches X=-1.764 and Y=-1.094, Blastoise Y=-1.248, Pikachu Y=-1.672, and Greninja X=1.073. The model outline is excluded, so these figures slightly understate clipping.

The contact sheet is a CPU-skinned geometry inspection with approximate per-triangle texture sampling, not a replacement art asset or the final Three renderer. No new imagery was generated for the game.

Source GLB URLs, revisions, attribution and hashes remain in `public/assets/pokemon-3d/provenance.json`. Each cue entry records its current GLB hash to prevent applying these sockets to a different rig. Primary distributions are [06wj/pokemon](https://github.com/06wj/pokemon) and [pokemon-party/3d-pokemon](https://github.com/pokemon-party/3d-pokemon). Official embedded UNITE video URLs were located, but automated video retrieval was throttled; no frame-accurate anime-footage claim is made. Timing measurements above come from the supplied skeletal clips, while move behavior comes from the cited official descriptions.
