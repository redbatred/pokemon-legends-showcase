# V60 — big-win juice: tier ladder, coins, impact frames and a reveal lens

Request: drastically improve the normal-mode special Pokémon win, from the Poké Ball breaking the card through the arena award. Mock payouts, settlement and the 6.12 s timeline are unchanged; every label is presentation only.

## References

Parameters come from a study of slot win presentation, Balatro and general game feel:

- Balatro source mirror (`juice_up`, pitch per trigger, score flames, additive shake): https://github.com/balatro-src/balatro-src-reverse-engineering
- Stake Engine reference slot (tier ladder with intro/idle/outro, coin fountain escalation): https://github.com/engineio/web-sdk
- Trauma-squared shake after Eiserloh: https://bevy.org/examples/camera/2d-screen-shake/
- Hit-stop lengths: https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag
- Impact frames: https://blog.sakugabooru.com/glossary/impact-frames/ ; flash limit: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html

No reference artwork, footage or code is used in the product.

## Arena win (`WinCinema`)

- `WinJuice`: one pooled, procedural Pixi layer (no bitmap downloads). Embers drift through the arena; light gathers into the Pokémon's emitter during every charge; a muzzle flare marks each release; a hot point with a tail rides each attack from the emitter into the amount; every hit throws a pressure ring, sparks and Poké coins scaled by its weight. The held award keeps a coin rain (MEGA and above) and twinkles.
- `WinTierLadder`: lower tiers land on earlier strikes and shatter when the next arrives — MEGA shows BIG first; EPIC shows BIG, MEGA; LEGENDARY shows BIG, MEGA, EPIC. The awarded tier still lands only on the finisher. Each rung drops in at 3×, squashes and rings out with Balatro's `juice_up` curve.
- Score flames behind the amount grow with the count and the tier, then turn to the tier's award colour.
- `WinHeroLight`: a fan of light on a billboard behind the actor inside the three.js arena, so the Pokémon occludes its own backlight. It pulses on each strike and holds behind the final award.
- The whole lens takes each strike: a short punch-in plus additive trauma with a squared shake. The finisher gets one anime impact frame (white, dark with white lines, fading white); the lens strike at 1.72 s gets a single soft white. The two flashes are 4.4 s apart.
- Final title: tier palette (cool, gold, hot, white-gold), a slow per-letter drift and a travelling highlight.
- Sound: count ticks rise in pitch through the roll-up (`playSound` accepts `rate`), and each rung answers with a brighter stinger.

## Poké Ball reveal (`SpineReveal`, `Stage`)

- A reveal lens zooms the backdrop and the card world together: it pushes in as the ball flies, punches on the puncture, drifts in while the ball settles, punches again on the release, rides the charge, and is hidden by the arena before it resets. The HUD and bonus row step back to 14% while the lens is in.
- 85 ms hit-stop on the puncture.
- `RevealJuice`: puncture (flash, two rings, sparks, speed lines), bounce dust, light gathering into the sealed ball, release (standing shaft of light, rings, motes), grounded landing ring, and a muzzle flare for non-cinematic specials.

## Special pull (below 2× the pack price)

In live play the arena only opens for cards worth at least 2× the pack price (`winTier`), and `GUARANTEED_FIRST_SPECIAL` is off, so most Poké Ball reveals end in the in-place `SPECIAL PULL` award rather than the arena. That award now uses the battle lettering, drops in oversized and rings out, throws a pressure ring and sparks from the amount on every count beat, and pays the exact total in Poké coins. The reveal lens stays close through the count and clears before the amount travels to the total.

`/tests/browser/win-demo.html` is the actual game with a fixed demo pack and sound, playable by hand: `?set=ladder|special|big|mega|epic|legendary`, or `?cards=charizard:2000,blastoise:8000`. Fixture values are mocks on demo credits.

Reduced motion disables every new layer, the lens and the hit-stop. Turbo keeps its existing direct finish. Low graphics halves particle budgets.

## Review round: one light language, flat sheets retired

Feedback on the first pass: the light effects on the amount were liked; the older flat sheets were not, and flat 2D effects read wrong against a 3D-looking game. The pass was reworked so every beat is light with depth and each effect is caused by the one before it:

- Retired from the normal special win (files remain, unused): `WinAttackFrames`, `WinStadiumEntry`, `WinReturnCels`, the Spine pressure burst and title echo in `WinCinema`, `WinArtwork` in the special pull, `PokeballReleaseCels` and the flat pressure star in `SpineReveal`. `prepareWinEffects` now only uploads the shared light shapes; the cel atlases are no longer downloaded for this path.
- Scene change: the opening strike leaves the Pokémon as a growing bloom with rays and streaks rushing past the lens, floods the frame, then clears onto the arena (`lensBloom`). The return to the game is the same light in reverse.
- Attacks: gather at the emitter, a flare on release, a hot point with a tail and shed light into the amount; electric types also hold a live arc. Fire, water and ice keep their 3D stream.
- A tier banner now falls while the attack travels and lands on the frame of contact, so one hit causes the banner, the ring, the sparks and the coins. LEGENDARY's BIG rung belongs to the lens strike.
- Removed as noise: ambient embers, random twinkles, flat focus lines, the dark impact frame and the random coin rain. The held award pays from both ends of the amount in mirrored arcs (MEGA and above). Coins carry depth: near ones are larger, faster and pass in front.
- Score heat is soft additive flame again, white at the root and the element's colour at the tip.

Still flat and not yet reworked: the pack-total cinema (`PackWinCinema`, `ResultWin`), the Showdown bonus, and the in-scene `PokemonPower` attack drawings used by sub-2× special pulls.

Verification after the rework: TypeScript, production bundle, 203 files / 1,038 tests. One intermediate run under heavy machine load timed out in four unrelated tests; the rerun passed clean. Reviewed as contact sheets: Charizard BIG full flow (lens flood), Pikachu EPIC arena, Charizard special pull. Not reviewed at real speed or with sound.

## Layout and smoothness round

- Award text is laid out bottom-up from measured ink (`AWARD_INK`, `winCinemaGeometry`): prompt, amount, then the title rows. LEGENDARY WIN splits over two rows only on portrait screens; on wide screens it is one line. A test covers eight screen shapes (390×844 to 1920×1080) for both layouts. Before this, the two rows collided with each other and the amount on wide screens, and the amount covered the prompt.
- The amount stretches upward from a planted base on the finishing hit, and the title rides that stretch, so the two move as one body and never cross.
- Finisher hit-stop (`strikeClock`): the 3D performance holds its contact pose for 100–190 ms by tier while light, shake and sound continue, then rejoins the shared clock without a jump. The final title lands with the same punch curve as the tier banners.
- Particle layers are their own render groups and their sprite pools are allocated up front, so spawning no longer rebuilds the game's whole display list or allocates during a strike. Win lettering is drawn once, unseen, during the opening charge so no glyph is first rasterised on its own slam. The full-frame light is a sprite rather than a redrawn shape.
- The reveal lens pushes further on wide screens so the card reaches a similar share of the frame everywhere; the flat backdrop focus lines fade while the lens is in. The held award's mirrored coin arcs stay inside the frame.

Verification: TypeScript, production bundle, 203 files / 1,041 tests. Contact sheets reviewed for Mewtwo LEGENDARY at 800×571, 390×844 and 540×720 (count, finisher, hold, return), and the full game flow at 800×571. Not reviewed at real speed or with sound; the smoothness changes are structural and were not measured live.

## Scene-connection round: the payout lives in the arena

Feedback: the animations still read as an overlay; they should connect with everything in the scene. The parts that were screen-space moved into the three.js arena:

- `WinCoins`: the payout is instanced 3D coins. Each strike throws them from the point on the arena floor under the amount toward the Pokémon; they tumble, are lit by the scene's own lights, bounce with loss of height and grip, and lie flat where they stop, each with a contact shadow. The pile grows through the tiers and stays through the held award.
- A later strike sends its wave under coins that already landed: each hops as the wave reaches it, nearest first.
- The pressure wave is in the floor shader, centred on the point of contact, so it crosses the court in perspective and passes under the Pokémon. The amount also pools light on the floor, scaled by score heat.
- The strike light sits on the point of contact, so each hit lights the Pokémon and the floor from there. Gathering energy lights the Pokémon from its emitter for every element, not only the 3D stream attacks.
- The arena wears the win (`ChampionshipSet`): its light rings and lamps take each tier's colour as that tier lands, and every strike runs up the three rings in turn.
- The 2D layer keeps only what belongs to the lettering: the flare and sparks on contact, energy gathering and travelling, score flames and the full-frame light. The 2D coins, rings and mirrored fountains are removed from the arena win.

Verification: TypeScript, production bundle, 204 files / 1,045 tests, including coin physics (no coin passes the floor, every coin comes to rest, most land between strike and Pokémon, landed coins hop on a later wave, capacity recycling). Contact sheets: Blastoise MEGA and Mewtwo LEGENDARY at 540×720, Mewtwo LEGENDARY at 1280×720. Stepped cost stayed at 1–2 ms median per frame in the lab. The full game flow was not re-captured this round because the preview pane was not painting, which stalls the game's boot screen; the changed files are all inside the arena stage, which the isolated lab exercises through the same preparation path.

## Sound round: one designed set for the special win

Request: improve all the sounds heavily; the soft white streaks behind the amount looked out of place.

- `scripts/generate-win-audio-v14.mjs` synthesises 23 cues into `public/assets/audio-win-v14` (1.7 MB): oscillators, filtered noise, struck-metal partials and an algorithmic room; no samples or recordings. Short attack-critical cues are PCM WAV; the long ones are MP3 (ffmpeg). `cues.json` and `provenance.json` are written alongside.
- One sound per cause, in the same chain as the picture:
  - Poké Ball: `win-puncture` on the card break, `win-gather` rising into the release (played faster when the wait is shorter), `win-bloom` as the ball opens, `win-land` scaled by the Pokémon's weight.
  - Into the arena: `win-charge` rises through the opening charge, then `win-flood` starts on the release with its boom placed 0.64 s in, on the frame the lens floods.
  - Each attack: the Pokémon's own move voice plus `win-launch`; on contact `win-strike-1/2` by weight with `win-coins-1`, and the tier banner's `win-rung-N` on the same frame. Tiers step up a whole tone each (C, D, E).
  - Count: `win-tick`, pitched up through the roll-up.
  - Finisher: `win-charge` again, faster and higher, then `win-final-N` for the awarded tier (C, D, E, G; longer and wider for higher tiers) with a coin spray. The crowd follows as before.
  - Return: `win-return`. Special pull: `win-stamp`, pitched ticks, then `win-rung-1` with coins.
- Replaced on this path: `slam`/`credit` on strikes, `vault-break`/`slam`/`complete`/`wincollect` on the finisher, `charge` on releases, `flip` on return, and the extra `rare` at the cinematic count finish. Pokémon cries, move voices, ball bounce and crowd are unchanged.
- The score flames are removed. The amount now takes only a brief glow in its own colour on each strike; lasting light is the pool on the arena floor beneath it.
- Lab pages: `win-flow-lab.html?sound=1` plays with the speakers disconnected and logs every cue with the win age it fired at. Lab pages also resolve image decodes on load, because a tab that is not being painted defers decodes and stalled the game's start-up.

Verification: TypeScript, production bundle, 204 files / 1,045 tests. Cue log from the real flow confirms the order above for a MEGA win and a special pull, with reveal cues loaded in time (they are now preloaded with the special card). Spectrograms checked for structure and levels (peak −3.5 dBFS; RMS −16 to −24 dBFS). The sounds have not been listened to: their character and balance against the existing music and crowd are unverified.

## Poké Ball round: one 3D ball and one light chain, from the throw to the arena

Feedback: from the ball falling until the transition there were still flat 2D drawings that did not match the 3D style, and the beats were not connected.

- `PokeBall3D`: the visible ball is a three.js model rendered through the reveal's own renderer (`sharedPokemonRenderer`), so it uses the Pokémon's four-step toon ramp, lights and ink outlines. It has a lower shell with band and latch, a lid on a back hinge, a dark cavity that glows with the Pokémon's light, and a button that lights at the latch. It tumbles in flight, the spin bleeds off through the bounces (`ballSpin`), and it rests at three-quarters so the lid swings back and away. A second, clipped pass redraws the front of the lower shell over the forming Pokémon, so it rises out of the ball. Both passes are compiled during preparation. The Spine rig still carries the timing, position and aperture but is not drawn.
- The card is no longer punctured: it gives back on contact and springs upright, and the contact is light in the Pokémon's colour on the card face. Paper shards are removed.
- Formation: light flows from the open ball along a curve into the forming body, behind it, with a core at the chest. The Pokémon forms as a bright tint of its own light that keeps its 3D shading, lit by its energy light, instead of a flat white cut-out.
- Charge and attack: energy converges on the emitter with a core that grows with the charge, then a flare and a beam leave along the emitter's direction.
- Retired from this sequence (not drawn): the painted Spine ball and front shell, the flight, release, formation and conduit cel rigs, latch dots, the blue stroke trail (now a light streak with motes), entrance strokes and the in-place attack strokes from `PokemonPower`. Shadows are soft.
- Calmer light: smaller flash and no full-frame white on the card contact; no standing light shaft or large rings on the release; no large ring on the attack. The white peak into the arena is shorter, so the arena shows through the bloom. The special pull drops its 2D coins.

Verification: TypeScript, production bundle, 204 files / 1,045 tests. Close-up contact sheets of the real flow at 540×720 for Charizard: throw, contact, bounces, opening, formation, departure, charge, transition into the arena, and a special pull's attack. Not watched at real speed or with sound.

## Eye beat on the finishing strike, and the card hole restored

- The card hole is back as before: the ball punches through the artwork and the torn hole stays for the rest of the reveal. The card still gives back and springs upright on contact.
- Eye beat (real time, `EYE_BEAT` in `championshipBeat.ts`): from 5.12 s the lens closes on the eyes; at 5.22 s letterbox bars close to a strip across them (`eyeBars`); at 5.40 s each visible eye flashes with a four-point glint and a hairline horizontal flare, over a held glow in the Pokémon's light, with a new `win-glint` sound; at 5.60 s the bars open and the lens snaps back for the release at 5.78 s. The amount, tier banner and prompt step aside during the strip.
- The body slows for it (`strikeClock`): the 3D performance runs at 12% from 5.30 to 5.60 s, then snaps into the release pose exactly at 5.78 s, then holds on contact as before. The lens, light and sound run in real time; `WinCinemaStage.render` takes both clocks.
- Finding the eyes: eyelid bones pivot deep in the skull (Charizard, Mewtwo, Pikachu, Gengar...), which is why the first version framed the forehead. The eye meshes are now measured once per shot (visible meshes only; some files carry hidden alternate-form eyes), split by side of the face, and carried with the head bone. Models with eye locators (EffEye01/02) use those; models with neither use a span across the head.
- Framing: the lens looks along the face's forward (perpendicular to the line between the eyes, toward the side the wide camera sees; the eyes' own gaze when the pivots sit just behind them), at eye level with a slight turn. The face surface depth along that line is measured once from outside, and the lens keeps that depth plus a gap, so it never ends up inside a muzzle, a brow or a round body. Each eye is ray-tested against the body; a hidden eye gets no glint. The lens never drops below the floor.

Verification: TypeScript, production bundle, 204 files / 1,047 tests (new: slow-motion clock reaches the release exactly and snaps faster than real time; bars only during the eye shot). Eye shot reviewed as stills for Charizard, Mewtwo, Blastoise, Pikachu, Gengar, Lugia and Pidgeot, and the full beat in sequence for Mewtwo. Not watched at real speed or with sound.
