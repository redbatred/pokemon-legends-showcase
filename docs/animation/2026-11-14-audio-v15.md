# Audio v15 — every base cue rebuilt, plus the legendary-capture set

Request: improve the sounds for everything in the game. v14 did this for the special win; v15 does it for the 23 base cues the rest of the game plays (Part A) and adds 19 new `cap-*` cues for the ball-throw capture bonus (Part B). Pokémon moves, cries, crowd reactions, music, `vault-*` and `win-*` are untouched.

**The result was measured, not listened to.** Nobody has auditioned these files. Every number below comes from the generator's meter; the first real check is a person playing the game with sound on.

## What ships

- `scripts/generate-game-audio-v15.mjs` — the only source. Oscillators, filtered noise, struck partials, short decorrelated reflections and an algorithmic room. No samples or recordings.
- `public/assets/audio-game-v15/` — 42 files (17 WAV, 25 MP3; 2.0 MB: 0.9 MB base, 1.0 MB capture), `cues.json` (same shape as v14, plus optional `marks`) and `provenance.json` (every measurement, the file each base cue replaces and its measurements, PCM hashes).
- `src/game/audio.ts` — the v15 manifest is spread last into `cues`, so it wins for the 23 base names; `GameSound` joins the `Sound` union; `preloadCaptureAudio()` awaits the 19 `cap-*` cues. Nothing else changed. Older folders are still imported and still on disk; `audio-v12`, `audio-v9` and `audio-v7` no longer supply any cue that plays.

## Direction

- Every cue is layered: a transient, a body, a tail.
- Key of C major, as in v14 (`ROOTS` C, D, E, G; C major blooms). Ticks sit on G, chords on C, tension on the dominant (G, D, B), so a chain of flips, ticks and a win resolves instead of clashing.
- Rounded top: a fourth-order low-pass at 9 kHz on every master (7.6 kHz on foil and the exit whoosh), and no partial is voiced above 8 kHz. Largest share of energy above 8 kHz in any file: 1.7%.
- Tight low end: a 32 Hz high-pass on every master, higher on light cues. Impacts carry their weight in the mids (a 700–900 Hz body and a 2–3 kHz crack) with a short sub thump under them, so they survive a phone speaker.
- Width without hard panning: layers are made twice from fresh noise or opposite detune and set a little either side, then four short reflections per ear at unequal delays and, on larger cues, a small room. Nothing is panned past ±0.5. Stereo correlation runs from 0.59 (`cap-presence`) to 0.98 (`credit`).
- No clicks: every file starts and ends with 6 ms of digital silence, every layer ends on a 2 ms taper, and each master is faded under a raised cosine.

## Measurements

The meter runs on the shipped file; MP3s are decoded again first.

- **Loudness** is the loudest 400 ms after ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting. A shorter file is measured against the full window, so brief cues compare by energy.
- **Small** is the same after a 400 Hz second-order high-pass, standing in for a phone speaker.
- **Played** is the mean of the two plus `20·log10(gain)`: where a call at gain 1 lands in the mix.
- **Gain, Part A**: set so *played* equals that of the file replaced. Older mono files are measured 3.01 dB down, because Howler puts a stereo panner on every voice and a centred mono source reaches each ear at −3 dB; the new files are stereo and pass at unity.
- **Gain, Part B**: set to reach a level chosen per cue against the v14 set (v14 strikes play near −19 LUFS, its flood at −13, its tick at −34).

The run fails if any file peaks above −1 dBFS, if the first or last 5 ms rise above −50 dBFS, if DC exceeds 0.0005, if more than 3% of energy sits above 8 kHz, or if a base cue plays more than 2 dB from the file it replaces on either loudness figure. All 42 pass: peaks −3.3 to −2.6 dBFS, edges at digital silence for WAV and below −63 dBFS for decoded MP3, DC at most 0.00009.

## Things that differ from the brief, or that the next person should know

1. **The durations in the brief were not the durations in the game.** Most came from the v4 manifest, but nine base cues were already being served from `audio-v12` and two from `audio-v9`, and those files are much shorter: `ui` 0.12 s, `flip` 0.26, `normal` 0.32, `rare` 0.65, `complete` 0.72, `credit` 0.10, `win` 0.82, `wincollect` 0.24, `land` 0.16, `packrustle` 0.25. v15 follows the brief (within its ±20%) for file length and keeps the *onset* where the old effective file had it, so nothing fires earlier or later than before. The longer tails have two side effects: a scene voice holds the music duck until its file ends, so music stays ducked 0.2–0.8 s longer after `flip`, `normal`, `rare`, `win`, `wincollect`; and tails occupy voice slots a little longer (the 8-voice cap steals the oldest first, which will be a silent tail).
2. **The interface cues were, and still are, very quiet.** `ui`, `credit`, `land`, `flip`, `wincollect` and `packrustle` play at −47 to −57 LUFS; the cinematic cues play at −20 to −33. That is inherited and matched, not chosen. The old files were clicks a few milliseconds long; the new ones are soft and pitched at the same energy. If they turn out too discreet under music, raise their `gain` in the generator's `trim` (dB) or multiply the manifest value: ×2 is +6 dB.
3. **`cap-merge` chimes at 0.80 s, not ≈0.9.** `ShowdownSymbolsView.celebrate` starts the cue at t = 0.45 and fuses at 1.25. It also plays `rare` at the fuse, which now doubles the chime.
4. **`cap-hold` is 1.30 s, not ≈1.4**, and stops at 1.29 s: `THROW.verdict − THROW.hold` is 1.30 s.
5. **`cap-enter` booms at 1.00 s** as briefed, but `celebrate` starts it at `FLOOD − 0.25`, which puts the boom 0.37 s after the veil is full and 0.33 s after `END`. Start it that much earlier if the boom should meet the full flood.
6. **`cap-card` snaps at 0.60 s** as briefed, but `CaptureEncounter` begins the flip at `REWARD.flip` = 0.72 s and plays the base `flip` at 0.88 s, so there are two snaps.
7. **`cap-ready`, `cap-merge` and `cap-enter` are played from `ShowdownSymbolsView`, before or while `prepareCapture` loads them.** `audio.ts` drops a cue that is not loaded rather than playing it late. `preloadCaptureAudio()` needs to be called earlier, for example when the first bonus ball is collected.
8. Base download grows from 0.38 MB to 0.88 MB. Short struck cues are PCM for sample-accurate attacks; soft or long cues are MP3 at LAME `-q:a 2`.
9. `suspense` and `charge` both run past the moment they build to, as the old files did. `charge` would fit better if `PackWinCinema` passed `rate: 1.3/0.74`, the way `WinCinema` fits `win-charge`.

## Regenerate

```
node scripts/generate-game-audio-v15.mjs                    # all 42 cues, both manifests
node scripts/generate-game-audio-v15.mjs --only ui,flip     # a few cues; prints the meter, leaves the manifests alone
node scripts/generate-game-audio-v15.mjs --measure some.mp3 # the same meter on any file
```

Needs `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` (MP3 encoding, and decoding the older files to match against). Each cue draws random numbers from its own seeded stream, so the output is identical run to run and editing one cue never changes another; two consecutive runs here produced byte-identical folders with ffmpeg 8.1.2. If an older file is ever deleted, the generator falls back to the measurement of it stored in `provenance.json`.

To change a cue, edit its `cue(...)` block: `seconds` is the whole file, `replaces` the file to match, `level` the played loudness for a capture cue, `trim` an optional offset in dB. Run with `--only` until the meter is happy, then run once without it.

## After the first pass

The ten interface cues (`ui`, `flip`, `normal`, `rare`, `complete`, `credit`, `win`, `wincollect`, `land`, `packrustle`) carry `trim:4` in the generator: they play 4 dB above the loudness of the files they replace. They changed from bright clicks to soft pitched sounds, which read quieter at equal energy. The loudness guard checks against the intended trim. This was a judgement from the measurements, not from listening.

The capture bonus (the scene in `output/capture-motion-v25/`) keeps its own music, score and voices. Its light layer adds a few of these cues as accents at reduced gain: `cap-hit`, `cap-shut`, `cap-wobble-1..3`, `cap-lock` / `cap-break`, `cap-card`, `cap-mult`, and for the count `win-rung-*`, `win-coins-*`, `win-final-4`. The other `cap-*` cues (merge, enter, presence, ready, throw, absorb, drop, hold, flee, exit) are generated but not used.
